The Höllengebirge is a plateau, not a ridge. Between Lake Traunsee and
Lake Attersee lies a high plain where the summits seem to grow out of
the ground rather than rise from it. That's exactly what makes a
traverse possible: stay up top instead of going back down each time.

## Three summits, upward

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brennerin** | OE/OO-051 | 1,602 m | 6 points |
| **Brunnkogel** | OE/OO-033 | 1,708 m | 6 points |
| **Grünalmkogel** | OE/OO-217 | 1,821 m | 8 points |

The first two on the twelfth. The third early on the thirteenth.
Twenty points, and each summit a good hundred metres higher than the
last — the traverse climbs rather than rolls.

## The evening

By 21:23 the sun was already below the horizon. The lake lay as a
bright surface between the ridges, night-blue above it, the plain
below with its first lights. What looks like a thin strip in the
photo is all that's left of the daylight.

![Evening light over the lake, seen from the ridge](../../../assets/photos/abendlicht-ueber-dem-see.jpg)

Anyone still up here at this hour has made a choice.

## The night

The summit cross on the Grünalmkogel stands in guy wires like a
transmission mast — and is built from the same kind of lattice work.
Below it a tent, sleeping bags in the grass, someone already up and
looking east.

> There isn't much to say about it. You just have to set off early
> enough and stay late enough.

## The morning

Eight hours later, the same direction, from the other side of the
day. The sun comes up behind the ridge and lays rays across the dwarf
pines.

![Sunrise over the ridge, the lake in the distance](../../../assets/photos/sonnenaufgang-ueber-dem-grat.jpg)

Then the last summit — and at 1,821 metres, the highest of the round.
