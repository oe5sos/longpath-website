The Windhagkogel sits in the SOTA list at 1,334 metres, reference
OE/OO-070, locator JN67xu, four points. On paper, a manageable
affair.

## Where's the path?

**The signposting is poor** — and that's still the polite way to put
it. But something else mattered more: by late July the grass stands
hip-high, and the actual trail vanishes completely inside it. No
footprint, no groove, nothing to see. You walk through a meadow and
hope there's a path underneath.

![Climbing through hip-high grass, nothing of the path visible](../../../assets/photos/windhagkogel-hohes-gras.jpg)

**I wouldn't walk this without a navigation device.** Not because
it's dangerous, but because without any trace on the ground you
simply have no way to tell whether you're still on course. The route
on the device replaces what the grass hides.

## Equipment, light

This was a deliberately small kit — everything that went up the
mountain fits on one board:

![The complete kit laid out: KX2, LiFePO₄ battery, telescopic whip with coil, coax on a blue reel, a clamp mount, folding tripod legs, and microphone cable](../../../assets/photos/windhagkogel-ausruestung.jpg)

The **KX2** as the radio, plus a **LiFePO₄ at 12.8 volts and 4
amp-hours** — 51.2 watt-hours, a good 200 grams, and generously
sized for an afternoon. As antenna a **telescopic whip with loading
coil**, one radial as counterpoise, a short thin coax on a reel.
Plus a clamp mount and folding legs so the whip stands without a
tree.

The **microphone** is from **OE5JFE** — thanks again to Jo. The
orange button in the cable is exactly what you want on a mountain:
you find it without looking.

## On top

40 metres, **7.190 MHz SSB**, log kept on paper. No screen, no
computer, no connection to the network.

![The KX2 on 7.190 MHz LSB, the handwritten paper log beside it](../../../assets/photos/windhagkogel-kx2-40m.jpg)

And then, right in the middle of it, **two minutes of hail**.
Unannounced, not threatening, just a shower that swept over the
summit and was gone again. Long enough to stow everything under a
jacket, short enough to carry on right after.

Four points for a mountain that takes your path away and then
throws ice at you for good measure. Fair enough.
