The Traunstein is the mountain you always have in view from Gmunden.
**1,691 metres**, reference **OE/OO-038**, six points — and it doesn't
sit somewhere in the hinterland but **right on the lakeshore**. From
the water to the summit cross it's about 1,270 metres of ascent,
with no foothills in between. That's why it looks so disproportionately
huge from below.

![Wide panorama from the Traunstein across the whole of Lake Traunsee, the sheer limestone face on the left, Gmunden and the alpine foreland on the right](../../../assets/photos/traunstein-panorama.jpg)

## Setting off early

The climb started in the dark. Shortly after seven the sun already
stood over the opposite slope, but the path still lay in shadow —
and halfway up there's this bench, set exactly into the gap between
two spruces.

![A wooden bench halfway up, the view across Lake Traunsee to the Höllengebirge behind it](../../../assets/photos/traunstein-bank-morgens.jpg)

At the summit, the other direction: Gmunden at the lake's outflow,
the alpine foreland beyond it out to the haze on the horizon.

![View from the summit across Gmunden and Lake Traunsee out into the alpine foreland](../../../assets/photos/traunstein-blick-gmunden.jpg)

## Chiemgau on Air

That Saturday I took part in the **first edition of "Chiemgau on
Air"** — a SOTA activation day launched by the DARC local chapter of
Traunstein. A nice coincidence: the chapter shares its name with the
mountain I was sitting on, though it's a good hundred kilometres
further west, in Bavaria.

The goal was to occupy **all 42 Chiemgau SOTA summits** in a single
day, from the Sattelberg at 820 metres to the Sonntagshorn at 1,961.
In the end it was **31 of 42**, done by **35 radio amateurs from
Germany, Austria and South Tyrol**. The special callsign **DL25COA**
went on air alongside it.

The Traunstein itself isn't part of the Chiemgau — on a day like
this, though, that's more of an advantage. With thirty summits on
the bands at once, you don't have to search long: you turn the dial
and pick up one summit-to-summit contact after another.

## The station

Set up was the small kit: a **telescopic whip with loading coil** on
a low tripod, a radial laid over the rock, a short coax run to the
radio.

![The telescopic whip with coil on a tripod at the rocky summit, the view into the alpine foreland behind](../../../assets/photos/traunstein-antenne.jpg)

Operating was on the **KX2 on 20 metres**, 14.219 MHz SSB, plus a
detour to 40.

![The KX2 on 14.219 MHz USB, the S-meter reading](../../../assets/photos/traunstein-kx2-20m.jpg)

## What came of it

**32 contacts.** On the map in the SOTA database it stretches right
across the continent: **Wales** in the west, **Pamplona** in northern
Spain, **Turin** and **Milan** in the south, **Wrocław** in the north,
and out at the far edge a call from **Ukraine**. Most of it on
14 MHz, some on 7.

Six points for a mountain I see from home every day. That's the
most pleasant kind of activation there is.
