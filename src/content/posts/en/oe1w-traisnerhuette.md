In September 2025 I got to visit the contest station **OE1W**. It
sits at the **Traisnerhütte** on the Muckenkogel above Lilienfeld, at
**1,313 metres**, locator **JN77TX**. Radio amateurs have been guests
there for more than twenty years.

The climb up was good, the weather perfect.

![The Traisnerhütte on the Muckenkogel, the contest station's masts all around](../../../assets/photos/oe1w-traisnerhuette.jpg)

## The contest

The **IARU Region 1 VHF contest** on 144 MHz ran from **Saturday
16:00 to Sunday 16:00** — twenty-four hours without a break. In the
end the log book counted **1,910 logs from 35 countries**.

OE1W's result:

**1,038 contacts** were logged, **976 of them counted after
cross-checking** — an error rate of 6.4 percent, a very clean figure
for a night of continuous operating. That worked out to **409,912
points** and with it **rank 6 of 193 multi-op stations** across all
of Region 1.

The longest contact went to **G4PIQ in England — 1,127 kilometres**
on two metres.

## The antennas

This is the part that hits you first on arrival. Five antenna
groups stand up there:

```
8 × 9 elements      4 × 9 elements      2 × 18 elements
4 × 4 × 8 elements                      3 × 12 elements
```

Together that's more than thirty Yagis and, counting up the
elements, a good three hundred of them. Plus **one kilowatt** at the
final amplifier.

![Stacked Yagis one above another on the mast, veil clouds behind](../../../assets/photos/oe1w-antennen-gruppe.jpg)

That makes OE1W one of the most modern VHF contest stations in
Europe. It's not any single piece of gear that shows it, but the
fact that everything fits together: antennas, preamps, feed lines,
rotators, computers.

![The operating positions inside, the cable runs along the wooden wall above](../../../assets/photos/oe1w-arbeitsplaetze.jpg)

## Twenty-four hours

A contest spanning a full day has its own rhythm. Saturday evening
is loud, at night things thin out and the contacts reach further,
and by Sunday morning everything comes back once more. When the sun
sits low, you finally see how much is hanging in the air up there.

![The big mast with the stacked groups in flat light, the flag and the hut's roofs beside it](../../../assets/photos/oe1w-abendsonne-mast.jpg)

On Sunday morning a closed sea of fog lay in every valley, and the
masts stood free above it.

![Two antenna masts above a closed sea of fog, the Alps behind](../../../assets/photos/oe1w-masten-ueber-nebel.jpg)
