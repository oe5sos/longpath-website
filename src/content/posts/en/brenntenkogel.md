A pleasant mountain that almost nobody climbs. The Brenntenkogel
stands northwest above Ebensee, 1,135 metres, locator JN67vt — four
points in the SOTA scoring. From the valley you can barely make it
out as a summit of its own; it's more the high point of a long,
forested ridge.

Which is exactly why nobody goes there. Not one other person on the
whole route.

## The path

Down low, everything's fine. Forest track, trail, straightforward.
**In the upper section the waymarking practically stops** — what's
still there is old and rarely where you need it. You end up going by
terrain and by feel, and both work here because the ridge itself
sets the direction.

Still: anyone doing this for the first time should have the route on
their device. There are spots where two plausible continuations sit
side by side and only one of them actually goes anywhere.

![A rocky rib along the ridge, the forest falling into the valley on the left, the mountains above Lake Traunsee in the distance](../../../assets/photos/brenntenkogel-felsrippe.jpg)

The forest has been logged in stretches. Looks rough, but it opens
the view — halfway up you suddenly stand free above the valley,
something that never happens on a ridge covered in unbroken forest.

## Raspberries

And where the forest was cleared, the raspberries stand. **Right
along the path**, dense bushes for hundreds of metres, ripe in July.
It's hard to get past them without slowing down.

![Dense raspberry canes with ripe fruit right beside the trail](../../../assets/photos/brenntenkogel-himbeeren.jpg)

That's the real reason to come here in July.

## On top

Short business at the summit: **IC-705, 145.500 in FM**, a
omnidirectional whip on a pole. Plus a look at the SWR sweep before
starting — with an antenna that spent half the climb in the
backpack, that's a minute not wasted.

![The IC-705 on 145.500 MHz FM, the SWR sweep on screen, the small antenna analyser beside it](../../../assets/photos/brenntenkogel-ic705.jpg)

Down into the valley you look southeast to Ebensee, the range
continuing behind it. A summit that gives back surprisingly much for
its height — and leaves you the whole afternoon to yourself.
